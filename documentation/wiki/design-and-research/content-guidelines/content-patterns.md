# Plain language guide

### What is plain language?

Simply put, plain language is language our audience understands and feels comfortable with. For our framework, we define it as language that is clear, concise, useful, and accessible.&#x20;

It’s also the law. [The Plain Writing Act of 2010](https://digital.gov/resources/plain-writing-act) defines plain writing as “writing that is clear, concise, well-organized, and follows other best practices appropriate to the subject or field and intended audience.”&#x20;

#### Why plain language?

If our ultimate goal is to “build the simplest, most accessible, and easiest-to-use way to publish, find, apply for, award and manage Federal financial opportunities ever built, inside or outside the federal government,” then it’s imperative that we use plain language.&#x20;

Plain language has many benefits, including:&#x20;

* Improved clarity: Plain language removes ambiguity and jargon. It makes it easier for readers to grasp the intended message quickly and accurately.
* Increased trust: When information is presented in a straightforward manner, it builds credibility and makes the organization appear more trustworthy.
* Time and cost savings: By reducing confusion and the need for clarification, plain language saves time for both writers and readers, ultimately leading to cost reductions.

#### Step 1: Think about your audience

We want to write for our audience using terminology they’re familiar with, answering their questions directly, and referring them to resources when needed.&#x20;

When writing for applicants, we should should keep the following pain points in mind:

* **Registration friction:** Complex multi-system registration
* **Information overload:** Difficulty finding relevant opportunities among thousands
* **Deadline panic:** Last-minute submissions causing system crashes
* **Format confusion:** Inconsistent requirements across different agencies
* **Status anxiety:** Lack of transparency in review processes

#### Step 2: Make it clear

**Put the main idea first**

Figure out the main idea of what you need to communicate and put that first. Write your main idea or instruction before any exceptions or special circumstances.

| **INSTEAD OF**                                                                                                                                               | ✅ **TRY THIS**                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Except in November and December due to the Thanksgiving and Christmas holidays, we will hold office hours on the third Wednesday of every month from 2-4 pm. | We’ll hold office hours on the third Wednesday of every month from 2-4 pm, except in November and December due to the Thanksgiving and Christmas holidays. |

**Keep your sentences and paragraphs to one topic**&#x20;

Long, complex sentences containing multiple phrases and clauses can confuse the reader. Break up long sentences and paragraphs up into multiple sentences or paragraphs, wherever possible.&#x20;

| **INSTEAD OF**                                                                                                                          | **✅ TRY THIS**                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Make sure to answer all required questions on the application because if you do not, it may take us longer to process your application. | Make sure to answer all required questions on the application. It may take us longer to process if you don’t answer all the required questions. |

If breaking a sentence into smaller ones makes the content sound awkward, choppy, and/or repetitive, it may be better to rewrite the sentence using a numbered list or bullet points of items.

* Use numbers if you are referring to specific steps that must be done in a particular order.&#x20;
* Use bullet points If your list of items does not have to be done in a specific order or is not a list of steps.

| **NUMBERED LIST**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | **BULLET POINT LIST**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <p>To make an account, follow these steps:<br></p><ol><li>Click the “New Account” button.</li><li>Enter your email address in the box labeled “email address.”</li><li>Enter a strong password in the box labeled “password.” The form will prompt you if your password does not meet the requirements.</li><li>Enter the password again in the box labeled “re-enter password.”</li><li>Click the “submit” button.</li></ol> | <p>You will need the following information when you fill out the application:<br></p><ul><li>Your full name as it appears on your paychecks or tax documents.</li><li>Your date of birth.</li><li>Your email address.</li><li>Your phone number.</li><li>Your employer’s name.</li><li>A phone number or email address we can reach your employer at.</li><li>The reason you need to take paid leave.</li></ul> |

**Use positive language to tell readers what you want them to do**

Positive language (telling your readers what you do want them to do) is simpler and less confusing than negative language (telling readers what you do not want them to do). Avoid double negatives, or sentences that involve two negative or negating words.&#x20;

| **INSTEAD OF**                                                            | **✅ TRY THIS**                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| If your application is incomplete, you will not receive an email from us. | We will only email you if your application is complete. |
| Do not upload forms other than the ones we tell you to upload.            | Only upload the forms we ask you to upload.             |

#### Step 3: Make it concise

**Avoid unnecessary words**

The federal plain language guidelines say that, “Wordy, dense construction is one of the biggest problems in government writing.” In many cases, you can “weed out” wordy sentences by looking for unnecessary or redundant words.&#x20;

When developing or critiquing content, ask yourself:

* Can I cut this word without changing the meaning?
* Does this word add value? Do I need it?

Modifiers like absolutely, actually, completely, really, quite, totally, and very are generally not necessary.&#x20;

| **INSTEAD OF**                                                                                                   | **✅ TRY THIS**                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| HUD and the FAA issued a **joint** report.                                                                       | HUD and the FAA issued a report.                                                                    |
| You can’t proceed until you’ve filled out each **and every** form.                                               | You can’t proceed until you’ve filled out every form.                                               |
| You can email us or use the secure online portal **in order** to submit more information about your application. | You can email us or use the secure online portal to submit more information about your application. |

**Need to know vs. nice to know**

On longer form content, figure out what information is ‘need to know’ vs. ‘nice to know.’ Often, the ‘nice to know’ content is background information or information that is mainly important to internal audiences. If it’s not related to the reader’s task at that moment, consider removing, linking to it if it exists elsewhere, or placing it at the bottom of the page.

#### Step 4: Make it useful

**Direct reader’s next action**

We should always make sure to provide guidance on the next action to take rather than throwing too many options at people, which can cause information overload. Useful content is practical and focused. &#x20;

**Minimize cross-referencing**

Cross-references are when you refer to another document that people must then look up, taking them away from the task at hand. Cross-referencing can also happen within a document (for example: “Refer to the ‘Eligibility’ section for a list of requirements”).

You can minimize this by organizing your writing so that related materials stay close together. Doing so also makes sure that people absorb the material in a logical order, making it more useful.

#### Step 5: Make it consistent

**Use the same term for the same idea**

Pick one term for one idea and stick to it. This is especially true for any terms you have to define in the text. Using a different term can make your audience wonder if you are referring to a different idea or concept.

**Follow the style guide**

Rules for capitalization, punctuation, date/time formats, and other elements should be applied to all content.

#### Step 6: Make it accessible

**Use common words instead of jargon and technical language, where possible**

Readers complain about jargon more than any other writing fault. Writers often fail to realize that terms they know well may be difficult or meaningless to their audience. Try to substitute everyday language for jargon as often as possible.&#x20;

**Define words where you use them**

If you can’t replace the jargon, provide the definition where you use it. Put the definition immediately after the first use of the word, or as close to it as possible. Don’t make audiences flip to a glossary or word bank to look up the definition.

**Keep definitions short and free of regulatory/statutory language**

You want your definitions to be as short and to the point as possible. Don’t include regulatory or statutory language, except in very specific circumstances where there is a reason to include the regulatory or statutory definition of a term.&#x20;

**Aim for reading level targets**

Replacing uncommon or jargon-y words with more common ones where possible will help people accomplish their task easier and build trust with Simpler. See the Reading Level Targets section for more detailed guidance on readability.

#### Step 7: Make it compassionate

**Speak directly to the reader**

Use “you” to refer to the individual reader. Using “you” makes your reader feel like you are speaking to them. Using “you” personalizes the document; if your reader feels like you are talking directly to them, they are less likely to feel lost.

| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Persons wishing to apply for grants must fill out this form. | You need to fill out this form to apply for the grant. |
